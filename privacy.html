Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 August 2026 · Applies to the Backflow iOS app.

The short version
  • Backflow has no account and no server of ours. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • Your testers, customers, sites, assemblies, reports and signatures are stored on your iPhone, and—if you are signed in to iCloud—in your own private iCloud database.
  • We cannot read your reports, your customer list, or your signature. We have no way to.
  • One third party is involved, and only for purchases: RevenueCat, which checks your App Store receipt using an anonymous ID it generates for your device.
  • Nothing is sold, rented, or used for advertising. The app does no tracking and contains no ad or third-party analytics SDK.

1. Who this policy is from

“We” and “us” mean the developer and publisher of the Backflow iOS app. You can reach us at mail@gorkemerbektas.com. “You” means the person using the app—typically a certified backflow prevention assembly tester.

2. What the app stores

Backflow is a record-keeping tool, so it necessarily holds detailed records. Everything in the table below is entered by you and stays under your control. It is stored on your device, not sent to us.

RecordFields it can contain
Tester profile Name, certification number, certification expiry date, company name, phone, email, and a drawn signature image
Test gauges Serial number, manufacturer, model, last calibration date, calibration interval
Water purveyors Name, state, submission email address, portal URL, form identifier, notes

A note about your customers’ information

Much of what you type into Backflow is other people’s information—site contacts, phone numbers, addresses. We never receive it, which also means we cannot manage it on your behalf. Where rules apply to the handling of that information, meeting them is your responsibility as the person who collected it.

3. Where it is stored

On your device

Records live in the app’s private storage area inside the iOS app sandbox. Larger items—signature images and generated report PDFs—may be written by the storage framework as separate files in the same sandbox. The app also writes a rolling local backup: after saves, a complete JSON snapshot of your records is written to the app’s Application Support folder, and the ten most recent snapshots are kept while older ones are deleted. These snapshots exist so a mistaken bulk delete or a bad upgrade is recoverable. They never leave the device on their own.

Deleting the app from your iPhone removes this on-device storage, including the snapshots. Export first if you want to keep your records.

In your own iCloud account

When you are signed in to iCloud, iOS syncs the app’s records through Apple’s CloudKit into the private database of the iCloud container iCloud.com.gelirmotoru.backflow, under your Apple Account. That is what makes your records appear on your other devices and survive a lost phone.

This is worth being precise about, because it is the part people most often assume works the other way round:

  • The data goes to your iCloud storage, not to a server we operate. We do not run one.
  • The private database is not visible to us. We have no administrative view of it, no key to it, and no ability to query it. We cannot retrieve your records for you, even if you ask.
  • While it is in iCloud, the data is handled under Apple’s terms and privacy policy for iCloud.
  • Deleting the app does not necessarily remove the iCloud copy. You can manage or remove app data stored in iCloud from the iOS Settings app, under your Apple Account’s iCloud storage settings.
  • Signing out of iCloud, or turning iCloud Drive off for the app, stops the sync. The records already on the device stay on the device.

4. Analytics

Backflow does not use a third-party analytics service. There is no Firebase, no Amplitude, no Mixpanel, no PostHog, and no equivalent in the app.

What the app calls “analytics” internally is a set of counters written to the device’s own system log using Apple’s logging framework. Those lines are visible to you on your own Mac if you attach the device to Console, and to no one else. They are not transmitted anywhere. The app makes no network request to send them.

The events are structural, not personal. In full, they record:

  • which onboarding step was reached,
  • that the subscription screen was shown, and from where,
  • that a trial or purchase was started, and for which product identifier,
  • that a named feature was used,
  • counts—how many items a screen was showing.

No names, no addresses, no readings, no report contents, and no device or advertising identifier are part of these events.

5. Purchases and RevenueCat

Subscriptions are sold by Apple through the App Store. We never see your payment details, your card, or your Apple Account credentials—Apple does not share them with developers.

To work out whether a subscription is active, the app uses RevenueCat, a subscription infrastructure provider. This is the one place where data leaves your device to a party other than Apple, so here is exactly what happens:

  • The RevenueCat SDK is initialised when the app launches.
  • We do not give it an identity for you. Because no user ID is supplied, RevenueCat generates an anonymous app user ID of its own and stores it on the device. It is a random identifier, not derived from your name, email, or Apple Account.
  • That anonymous ID, your App Store purchase receipt, and technical metadata about the device and store are sent to RevenueCat so it can answer one question: is the subscription entitlement active?
  • If you reinstall the app or reset the device, a new anonymous ID is usually generated. Restoring purchases works from the App Store receipt, not from anything we hold.
  • We see aggregate subscription figures. We do not see who you are.

RevenueCat processes this data as our service provider, under its own privacy policy at revenuecat.com/privacy.

6. Notifications

Backflow can remind you about assembly retest dates, gauge calibration expiry, and your own certification expiry. These are local notifications, scheduled by the app on your device from records already on your device. There is no push server, and nothing about your schedule is sent anywhere to produce them.

Reminder text can include a site or assembly name, so it may appear on your lock screen. You can change that in iOS Settings, or decline notification permission altogether—the rest of the app works without it.

7. Exporting and sharing

You can export a complete JSON archive, a CSV of your reports, or a PDF of an individual report, at any time, using the iOS share sheet. Export is free and is not restricted by a subscription.

One limitation worth knowing before you rely on the archive as a backup: the JSON archive contains your records—testers, gauges, purveyors, sites, assemblies and reports—but it does not include the drawn signature images or the stored copies of generated PDFs. Export the PDFs you need to keep separately.

When you share a file, it goes wherever you send it—Mail, Files, a purveyor’s portal, a messaging app. That destination is your choice, and from that point the receiving service’s handling applies, not ours. Email addresses shown in the app open your own mail app with a pre-filled message; nothing is sent until you send it.

8. What the app does not do

  • No account, no sign-up, no password, no login of ours.
  • No access to your location, camera, photo library, contacts, calendar, microphone, or health data. The app does not ask for these permissions because it does not use them.
  • No advertising, no ad SDK, no advertising identifier, no cross-app or cross-site tracking. The app’s privacy manifest declares tracking as disabled and lists no tracking domains.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing of your data with data brokers. There is no data to sell: we do not hold it.
  • No profiling and no automated decision-making about you.

9. What the App Store privacy label reflects

The app’s privacy manifest declares two collected data types, both tied to purchases:

  • Purchase history — not linked to your identity, not used for tracking; used for app functionality and to understand subscription performance in aggregate.
  • User ID — the anonymous RevenueCat identifier described in section 5; not linked to your identity, not used for tracking, used for app functionality.

The manifest also declares access to the standard iOS preferences store, used only for the app’s own settings.

10. Retention

Your records stay until you delete them. We do not expire, archive, or remove them, because we do not hold them. Local snapshots roll over automatically at ten. Purchase records held by Apple and RevenueCat are retained under their own policies.

11. Your controls

  • Stop syncing: turn iCloud Drive off for Backflow, or sign out of iCloud, in iOS Settings.
  • Remove the iCloud copy: manage app data in your Apple Account’s iCloud storage settings.
  • Delete records: delete individual sites, assemblies, or reports in the app.
  • Take your data with you: export JSON or CSV at any time, at no cost.
  • Remove everything local: delete the app.
  • Turn off reminders: decline or revoke notification permission in iOS Settings.

If you have a question about your data that these controls do not answer, write to mail@gorkemerbektas.com. Please note the honest limit: for anything stored only on your device or in your private iCloud database, we can explain and advise, but we cannot access, retrieve, or delete it for you.

12. Children

Backflow is a professional tool for certified testers. It is not directed to children and is not intended for their use.

13. Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that changes this policy—for example, if it ever gains a server, an account, or a new third-party service—this page will be updated and the date at the top will change. Material changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes.
